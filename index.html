FleetView

Find out how many robots your floor actually needs

Draw your warehouse, place a fleet, and watch it work a shift. FleetView simulates every robot tick by tick, then finds the fleet size where one more robot stops paying for itself.

Robots share aisles, and that is the hard part

Route each robot on its own and two of them will eventually want the same cell at the same tick. In a single-width aisle they meet nose to nose, neither can move again, and the floor quietly stops working.

FleetView plans the fleet together using Conflict-Based Search. It finds the conflict, branches on it, and re-plans the cheapest way around. Here that means parking one robot in the passing bay. Both panels run the real solver on the same corridor.

Solving the corridor

  1. 1

    Draw the floor

    Paint walls, racks, pick stations, deposit lanes and charge docks on a snapped grid. Import a PNG of a real plan and trace over it, or start from one of three built-in layouts.

  2. 2

    Run the fleet

    Set robot speed, turn cost, payload and battery. Orders arrive on a seeded random process. The simulation runs on a background thread, so a ten-thousand-tick day takes a couple of seconds.

  3. 3

    Read the payback

    Get throughput, utilisation, order latency and a congestion heatmap. Sweep fleet sizes to find the saturation point, then put hardware and labour costs against it.

What the numbers mean

Orders per hour
Completed orders scaled to an hour. This is the headline throughput number.
Utilisation
Share of time robots spend on real work. Low means too many robots; near 100% means no slack for a demand spike.
Latency p95
95% of orders finish faster than this. The mean hides the slow tail, so this is the number to build a delivery promise on.
Saturation point
The smallest fleet still within 95% of peak throughput. Past it, robots mostly queue behind each other.
